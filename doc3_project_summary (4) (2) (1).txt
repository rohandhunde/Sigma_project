PROJECT CLOSE-OUT SUMMARY
Prepared by: Anita Desai, Senior Project Manager
Distribution: Internal — Delivery Leadership, Finance, GTM

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Background and Context

The engagement with Northstar Health Systems began following an introductory meeting at the Healthcare
Innovation Summit in Chicago back in March of last year. After a competitive RFP process involving four
vendors, we were awarded the contract in late July 2023, with formal project kick-off taking place on the
fourteenth of August. The client is a mid-sized regional hospital network operating eleven facilities across
Ohio and Pennsylvania, with a central IT function based in Columbus.

The original scope covered three workstreams: an AI-powered prior authorisation assistant for their
revenue cycle team, a clinical document summarisation capability integrated into their Epic EHR
environment, and a compliance knowledge base covering CMS and HIPAA policy documents. All three
workstreams were included in the initial Statement of Work signed by their CTO, Dr. Marcus Webb, and
our own VP of Delivery, Sameer Krishnan.

Financial Summary

The total contract value was agreed at four hundred and twenty thousand US dollars. We invoiced in six
tranches across the project lifecycle: an advance of sixty-three thousand on signing, followed by five
milestone payments of varying size as each workstream delivered. The final invoice — covering the
hypercare period and knowledge transfer — was for thirty-eight thousand five hundred dollars and was
paid in full on the third of October this year, approximately two weeks ahead of the contractual due date,
which was a pleasant change from the payment behaviour on our previous healthcare engagement.

Total revenue recognised against this project was four hundred and eighteen thousand dollars, reflecting
a small credit note issued in September after a scope reduction on the compliance knowledge base
workstream — the client decided to limit indexing to CMS documents only, removing the HIPAA policy
layer which we had scoped separately. This was handled via Change Request CR-007, approved by both
parties on the ninth of September.

Delivery Performance

The prior authorisation assistant was the most technically complex component and also the one that
caused us the most difficulty. We had initially scoped a six-week delivery for the RAG pipeline and
chatbot interface, but encountered significant challenges with the client's legacy document store — a
mixture of scanned PDFs, Word documents, and some older formats we had not anticipated during
discovery. We ended up running two additional sprints and delivered the component eight weeks after
the original target date. The client was understanding but it created tension that persisted through the
rest of the engagement.

The clinical document summarisation workstream, by contrast, was our smoothest delivery of the year.
The Epic integration used a well-documented FHIR API, the client's IT team was well-prepared, and the
AI summarisation quality was high enough that the model exceeded our accuracy threshold — ninety-one
percent against a target of eighty-eight — on the first evaluation run. We went live on this workstream
on the twenty-second of March, six days ahead of plan.

The compliance knowledge base was delivered on time but with the reduced scope described above.

Overall on-time delivery rate for the engagement, accounting for the prior auth delay, was assessed at
seventy-three percent against our internal benchmark of eighty-eight percent. This is below our standard
and will be documented in the post-mortem.

Team Composition

The core delivery team comprised seven people at peak: two senior engineers (Rohan Pillai and
Devika Nambiar), one ML engineer (Tariq Hussain), two QA specialists, a business analyst, and Anita
Desai as project manager. Rohan led the RAG pipeline work; Devika owned the Epic FHIR integration;
Tariq was responsible for the summarisation model fine-tuning and the prompt evaluation framework.
At various points we also drew on a fractional solution architect — approximately forty days across the
project — and one additional QA resource for the regression cycle in month seven.

Client Satisfaction and Next Steps

End-of-project NPS score from the client was eight out of ten, collected via our standard close-out
survey sent to five stakeholders on the client side. Dr. Webb's feedback was particularly positive about
the clinical summarisation capability. Their Revenue Cycle Director, Patricia Okafor, flagged the prior
auth delays in her written feedback and requested that any future engagement include a more rigorous
document discovery phase upfront.

We have a verbal commitment from Dr. Webb for a follow-on engagement covering AI-assisted clinical
coding and denial management, estimated at approximately two hundred and fifty thousand dollars.
The SOW for this work is being drafted by our sales team and is expected to be ready for client review
by the end of this month. If signed, it would represent our largest single healthcare contract to date.

Lessons Learned

The main lesson from this engagement is that document discovery for RAG projects in healthcare
environments requires dedicated time and should not be compressed into general discovery. We will be
revising our standard healthcare engagement template to include a mandatory two-week document audit
phase before any RAG pipeline scoping is committed. Tariq has agreed to lead the development of
a document readiness checklist that can be used on future engagements.

The second lesson is that FHIR API integrations, when the client IT team is well-prepared and the
documentation is current, can actually go faster than scoped. We should consider this when estimating
similar workstreams and not apply our standard thirty percent buffer on Epic FHIR work.
